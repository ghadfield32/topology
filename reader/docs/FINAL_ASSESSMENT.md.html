Final assessment — independent work

Do this without opening the solutions. Afterwards correct errors and solve changed variants after a delay. Suggested rubric: 100 points total, at least 85 points, with every critical conceptual error corrected. Passing code alone does not pass this assessment.

A. Foundations (20 points)

  1. For f(x)=x²−1 on R, find f⁻¹((0,3]) and explain every endpoint. (5)
  2. Negate: for every ε>0 there exists δ>0 such that every pair with distance less than δ has output distance less than ε. (5)
  3. Give a continuous bijection that is not a homeomorphism and identify the failed inverse-image check. (5)
  4. Explain finite discrete data versus a sampled continuous shape without using the phrase “the points are already a circle.” (5)

B. Algebra and homology (25 points)

  1. Find kernel/image and rank/nullity of A=[[1,1,1],[0,1,1]] over F₂. (5)
  2. Prove ∂∂=0 for simplices using the paired-face argument. (5)
  3. A connected graph has six vertices and eight edges. With no filled triangles, compute β₀, β₁ and Euler characteristic. (5)
  4. Explain how adding 2-simplices can change this answer and which matrix you would examine. (5)
  5. Distinguish a tetrahedron boundary and filled tetrahedron in β₀, β₁ and β₂. (5)

C. Persistence and reliability (30 points)

  1. For four corners of a square of side two in our Rips convention, derive all H₀/H₁ positive or essential intervals. (10)
  2. Explain a filtration cutoff that would make its H₁ interval look infinite and why this interpretation is limited. (5)
  3. Compute the bottleneck distance from {(1,5)} to the empty finite diagram. (5)
  4. State and justify an applicable fixed-complex stability bound, distinguishing the easy inclusion proof from algebraic stability. (5)
  5. Give one stable but scientifically misleading data pattern and explain why stability is insufficient. (5)

D. Reproducible application (25 points)

  1. Design a motion-window experiment whose split avoids athlete/session and future-information leakage. (10)
  2. Explain the digits capstone's exact metric/filtration/field/representation and split limitations. (5)
  3. Report a negative TDA result honestly and identify what new evidence would be required to reverse it. (5)
  4. Reproduce one exact software check and one real-data result, preserving IDs, versions and limitations. Explain why a missing optional library is not a pass. (5)
