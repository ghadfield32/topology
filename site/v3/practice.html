00.Q1 · Stage 00

Explain observation, feature, label, and dataset using one handwriting image.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

An observation is one 8×8 image. Its 64 pixel intensities can be features. The written digit 0–9 is its label. The dataset is the collection of such images and labels. A pixel can instead become an observation in a different, explicitly chosen analysis.

00.Q2 · Stage 00

What do the two numbers in an array shape mean? What does X[:, 0] select?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

For a matrix the first number counts rows and the second counts columns. The colon selects every row; zero selects the first column. A 150×4 matrix is not a four-image dataset.

00.Q3 · Stage 00

Predict whether `3 == 3` and `3 == 4` are true; explain why assignment is different.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The comparisons evaluate to True and False. a = 3 changes the variable binding and does not ask a yes/no question. a == 3 tests the current value.

00.Q4 · Stage 00

Why do we run an exact synthetic example before a real-data experiment?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

An exact example provides an expected answer independent of the program. Real data exercise the method but usually do not reveal the true topology directly. Neither alone provides every kind of validation.

00.Q5 · Stage 00

What does a passed software test establish, and what does it not establish?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

It establishes that a specified check passed on the tested inputs in the executed environment. It does not certify all possible inputs, statistical significance, scientific usefulness, or the learner’s understanding.

00.Q6 · Stage 00

Find the digits split sizes in data/manifest.json and explain why they are not the original UCI split.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

There are 1,077 training, 360 validation, and 360 test images. All are from scikit-learn’s 1,797-image snapshot, which corresponds to the original UCI test set. This new split is per image; it cannot establish separation by writer.

01.Q1 · Stage 01

Find f⁻¹([1,9]) for f(x)=x² on R. Explain the endpoints.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The inverse image is [-3,-1] ∪ [1,3]. Solving 1≤x²≤9 yields 1≤|x|≤3, and equality is allowed.

01.Q2 · Stage 01

Negate: “For every radius r>0 there exists a sample with property P(r).”

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

There exists a radius r>0 for which no sample has P(r). The quantifiers both reverse and the inner property is negated.

01.Q3 · Stage 01

Prove f⁻¹(A ∩ B)=f⁻¹(A) ∩ f⁻¹(B).

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

x belongs to the left side exactly when f(x) is in both A and B; that is equivalent to x belonging to both inverse images. The equivalence proves both inclusions.

01.Q4 · Stage 01

Prove that congruence modulo three is an equivalence relation.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

a−a=0 is divisible by 3. If a−b=3k, then b−a=−3k. If a−b=3k and b−c=3l, then a−c=3(k+l). These prove reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity.

01.Q5 · Stage 01

Find the kernel and image of [[1,2],[2,4]] over R.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The map sends (x,y) to (x+2y)(1,2). Its image is span{(1,2)} and its kernel is span{(-2,1)}. Rank and nullity are both one.

01.Q6 · Stage 01

Explain why the binary three-by-three matrix in the lesson has different ranks over R and F₂.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Over F₂, row 3 equals row 1 plus row 2, while the first two are independent, so rank is two. Over R its determinant is -2, not zero, so rank is three. The scalar arithmetic changes linear dependence.

01.Q7 · Stage 01

Why does having four measured columns not prove that a biological population is a four-dimensional manifold?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Four columns specify an ambient representation. They do not establish local Euclidean structure, intrinsic dimension, sampling adequacy, or absence of constraints and noise.

01.Q8 · Stage 01

Explain the difference between a list of observations with duplicates and the set of distinct measurement vectors.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The list preserves measurement multiplicity and sample identity. The set collapses equal vectors. Zero feature distance between distinct observation IDs is possible; later we describe this as a pseudometric on IDs.

02.Q1 · Stage 02

Check all metric axioms for d(x,y)=|x−y| on R.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Absolute values are nonnegative, |x−y|=0 exactly when x=y, |x−y|=|y−x|, and |x−z|=|(x−y)+(y−z)|≤|x−y|+|y−z|.

02.Q2 · Stage 02

Give a numeric counterexample showing squared Euclidean distance is not a metric.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Use 0,1,2: (0−2)²=4 while (0−1)²+(1−2)²=2.

02.Q3 · Stage 02

List all open sets of the discrete and indiscrete topologies on {0,1}.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Discrete: empty, {0}, {1}, {0,1}. Indiscrete: empty and {0,1}. Both satisfy the axioms.

02.Q4 · Stage 02

Explain why (0,1] is open in the subspace (0,2) or determine that it is not.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

It is not open. Every subspace neighborhood of 1 includes numbers slightly larger than 1 and less than 2, which are outside (0,1]. The excluded point zero is not the obstacle.

02.Q5 · Stage 02

Prove every finite set of distinct metric points is discrete.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

For each x with at least one other point, take half the minimum of its finitely many positive distances to other points. The corresponding ball contains only x. A one-point space is already discrete. Every subset is a union of these open singletons.

02.Q6 · Stage 02

If two different sample IDs have the same features, is Euclidean distance on IDs a strict metric?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

It is a pseudometric on the IDs: distinct IDs can have distance zero. On the set of distinct coordinate vectors it is a metric.

02.Q7 · Stage 02

Explain why equal topology does not imply equal persistence diagrams in distance units.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

A topology remembers which sets are open, not the numerical thresholds at which edges enter a proximity complex. Uniform scaling preserves topology while scaling all distance-based birth and death values.

02.Q8 · Stage 02

Run the real-data metric comparison and state which preprocessing decision could change the result.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The saved stage report records the actual raw and standardized neighbor selections. Rescaling features, using a different metric, changing the observation unit, or adding labels can change them. Do not invent a universal interpretation from one ranking.

03.Q1 · Stage 03

Prove the composition of two continuous functions is continuous.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

For U open in the final codomain, g⁻¹(U) is open by continuity of g, then f⁻¹(g⁻¹(U)) is open by continuity of f. This set is the inverse image under the composition.

03.Q2 · Stage 03

Give a homeomorphism from (0,2) to (5,9) and its inverse.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

f(x)=2x+5; f⁻¹(y)=(y−5)/2. Each is continuous, and their compositions are identities on the declared intervals.

03.Q3 · Stage 03

Explain the continuous-bijection counterexample using two finite topologies.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The identity from discrete {a,b} to indiscrete {a,b} is continuous because only empty and whole-target inverse images must be checked. The reverse identity fails for the open target singleton {a}.

03.Q4 · Stage 03

Why does a path-connected space have to be connected?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

If a separation existed, choose one point in each part and a path between them. Its image would be connected as a continuous image of [0,1], yet would meet both parts of the separation, a contradiction. This uses the standard connectedness of intervals.

03.Q5 · Stage 03

Use the given cover to show (0,1) is not compact.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Each x in (0,1) lies in (1/n,1) for a sufficiently large n. A finite choice has a largest n=N; its union is (1/N,1), so it misses smaller positive points.

03.Q6 · Stage 03

Explain where compactness and the Hausdorff assumption enter the homeomorphism theorem.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Compactness makes closed subsets of the domain compact and their continuous images compact. The Hausdorff assumption makes those compact images closed. Thus the map is closed, which gives continuity of its inverse.

03.Q7 · Stage 03

Is projection R⁴→R² a homeomorphism? Could its restriction to a particular finite sample be bijective?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

It is not a homeomorphism of the ambient spaces because it is not injective: changing either discarded coordinate preserves the output. On a particular finite sample it may happen to be injective and hence bijective onto its finite image; that does not recover the missing ambient coordinates.

03.Q8 · Stage 03

Why can finitely many plotted points not establish continuity of a function on all of R?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

A function can agree with a continuous function at every sampled point and jump at an unobserved point. A universal claim needs assumptions or a proof beyond finite agreement.

04.Q1 · Stage 04

List the four group axioms and explain why associativity does not imply commutativity.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Closure keeps products in the set; associativity permits regrouping; an identity does nothing; every element has an inverse. Commutativity concerns reversing order, which is a different property and fails for many groups of transformations.

04.Q2 · Stage 04

For a square, give the inverse of a quarter-turn and the inverse of a reflection.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The inverse of a counterclockwise quarter-turn is a clockwise quarter-turn, or three counterclockwise quarter-turns. A reflection is its own inverse.

04.Q3 · Stage 04

Check the endpoints and fixed-subspace condition of the annulus deformation formula.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

At t=0 the factor is one. At t=1 it is 1/||x||, placing x on the unit circle. If ||x||=1 the factor stays one. The radius is a convex combination of ||x|| and 1, hence remains in [1,2].

04.Q4 · Stage 04

Why is the same formula invalid at the center of a disk?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

It divides by ||x||, which is zero at the origin. This is not a removable implementation inconvenience; the proposed formula is not defined on the full disk.

04.Q5 · Stage 04

Explain homeomorphism versus homotopy equivalence using the annulus and circle.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The annulus retracts onto its central circle, with reverse map inclusion; their composites are homotopic to identities. They are not homeomorphic: an annulus has two-dimensional surface neighborhoods, unlike the circle’s one-dimensional neighborhoods. Establishing such distinctions formally uses suitable invariants or local topology.

04.Q6 · Stage 04

What are the winding numbers of two forward turns followed by one reverse turn?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The net winding number is 2−1=1. Winding numbers add under concatenation and negate under reversal.

04.Q7 · Stage 04

Why does rotating a digit image not automatically produce a valid same-label training example?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The array permutation is a valid mathematical symmetry, but the recognition target can change. Augmentation must preserve the actual task label or explicitly transform it.

04.Q8 · Stage 04

What can and cannot be concluded when two spaces have equal Betti numbers?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Different Betti numbers obstruct homotopy equivalence. Equal Betti numbers alone do not prove homeomorphism or homotopy equivalence; homology is not a complete invariant.

05.Q1 · Stage 05

How many vertices, edges, and triangles occur in a filled triangle?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Three vertices, three edges, one filled triangle. The collection has seven nonempty simplices.

05.Q2 · Stage 05

Calculate the Euler characteristics of a triangle boundary, filled triangle, tetrahedron boundary, and solid tetrahedron.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The values are 0, 1, 2, and 1 respectively, from 3−3; 3−3+1; 4−6+4; and 4−6+4−1.

05.Q3 · Stage 05

What is wrong with storing a triangle but omitting one edge?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

It violates closure under faces, so the stored collection is not a simplicial complex. The validation function must reject it; calling closure is an explicit repair/construction choice.

05.Q4 · Stage 05

Explain abstract simplex versus geometric realization.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

An abstract simplex records a subset of vertex IDs and incidence relationships. A geometric realization assigns actual points and filled convex simplices satisfying intersection conditions.

05.Q5 · Stage 05

Why do corner-touching foreground pixels connect under this course’s model?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Closed squares include their vertices. Two squares sharing a corner have a nonempty intersection and form a connected union. A four-neighbor digital-connectivity convention would encode a different model.

05.Q6 · Stage 05

Why can a digit label not be used as the exact ground-truth Betti number of its thresholded image?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The label is a semantic class. The actual geometry depends on strokes, resolution, threshold and adjacency. It is a potential hypothesis about typical images, not an exact oracle for every image.

05.Q7 · Stage 05

For a torus with one open disk removed, calculate Euler characteristic using the surface formula.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

It is orientable with g=1 and b=1, so 2−2−1=−1. Removing the disk adds a boundary component; it is not a closed torus anymore.

05.Q8 · Stage 05

Explain why a graph containing a triangle and its clique complex can have different topology.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The graph may have only three edges and retain a loop. Its clique complex inserts the triangle face because the three vertices are pairwise adjacent, filling that loop.

06.Q1 · Stage 06

Explain the difference between Cₖ, Zₖ, Bₖ, and Hₖ.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Cₖ is every formal k-chain. Zₖ consists of chains killed by the boundary map. Bₖ consists of boundaries of (k+1)-chains. Hₖ identifies cycles differing by a boundary. Cₖ is not the homology group.

06.Q2 · Stage 06

Prove ∂₁∂₂=0 for one triangle and explain the general paired-face argument.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The triangle boundary is ab+ac+bc. Taking boundary gives a+b+a+c+b+c=0 over F₂. In general every codimension-two face arises through exactly two codimension-one faces; their contributions cancel.

06.Q3 · Stage 06

Compute β₀ and β₁ of a four-vertex tree with three edges.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The graph is connected, so β₀=1. A tree has no independent cycles; rank ∂₁=3 and β₁=3−3=0.

06.Q4 · Stage 06

Compute β₀ and β₁ of a connected graph with four vertices and five edges, with no triangles filled.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Rank ∂₁=4−1=3. With five edges and no 2-simplices, β₁=5−3=2. β₀=1.

06.Q5 · Stage 06

Explain why adding a triangle can decrease β₁ but never disconnect the existing graph.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

If the attaching triangular cycle represented a nonzero H₁ class, its new face makes it a boundary. Adding cells cannot remove existing paths, so it cannot split a connected component. A face does not always decrease β₁ if its boundary was already trivial.

06.Q6 · Stage 06

Derive the Euler–Poincaré identity using the rank formula.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Substitute βₖ=nₖ−rₖ−rₖ₊₁ into Σ(-1)ᵏβₖ. The rank terms cancel after an index shift; r₀=0 in ordinary homology and the next map above the complex is zero. What remains is Σ(-1)ᵏnₖ.

06.Q7 · Stage 06

What extra cells are needed to decide whether a two-dimensional cycle is a boundary?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

We need 3-chains and ∂₃. A tetrahedron boundary has a 2-cycle; adding the solid tetrahedron makes that cycle a boundary.

06.Q8 · Stage 06

Why do the results state F₂ and ordinary homology explicitly?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Changing the field can change ranks and lose or reveal information related to torsion. Reduced versus ordinary homology changes dimension-zero conventions. Stating both is necessary for comparable results.

07.Q1 · Stage 07

At what value does a Rips simplex enter under the declared convention?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

At the largest pairwise distance among its vertices; vertices enter at zero. The simplex is present whenever all its edges have entered.

07.Q2 · Stage 07

Why does the equilateral triangle have no positive H₁ Rips bar?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

All three edges and the filled triangular face enter at ε=1. Any edge-cycle birth and face death at that same value give a zero-length interval, not persistence over an interval of positive length.

07.Q3 · Stage 07

Derive the two Čech radii for that same triangle.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Each pair of equal-radius balls first touches when 2r=1, so r=1/2. The three centers are equilateral; their common circumcenter lies at distance 1/√3, which is the first common triple intersection radius.

07.Q4 · Stage 07

Prove the elementary inclusions Čech_r⊆Rips_2r⊆Čech_2r in Euclidean space.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

For a Čech simplex, choose a common intersection point p; each center pair is at most r+r apart. For a Rips simplex of diameter≤2r, choose one center p; it lies within 2r of every center, so it is in the common intersection of the radius-2r balls.

07.Q5 · Stage 07

Explain the good-cover assumption in a nerve theorem.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The nonempty finite intersections must satisfy the theorem’s contractibility requirements, with the appropriate cover hypotheses. An overlap pattern alone need not determine the union’s homotopy type.

07.Q6 · Stage 07

How many candidate edges and triangles exist for 20 observations?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

There are choose(20,2)=190 edges and choose(20,3)=1,140 triangles. Including the 20 vertices yields 1,350 candidate simplices through dimension two.

07.Q7 · Stage 07

Why does reporting H₁ from an edge-only complex answer a different question?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

An edge-only graph omits triangle boundaries that could make graph cycles trivial in a clique complex. Its homology can be correct for the graph and still wrong for the intended Rips complex.

07.Q8 · Stage 07

If an alpha value is 4 in squared-radius units, what is the radius? Is this automatically Rips ε=2?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The radius is sqrt(4)=2. That conversion only fixes alpha units. It does not make the alpha construction identical to a Rips construction at edge threshold two.

08.Q1 · Stage 08

Explain what the maps in a persistence module add beyond the vector-space dimensions.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The maps specify how classes continue, merge into combinations, or disappear. Identity and zero maps between equal-dimensional spaces give different persistence behavior even though the dimension sequence is identical.

08.Q2 · Stage 08

Write all positive or essential bars for the unit square in H₀ and H₁.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

H₀: three [0,1) intervals and one [0,∞). H₁: one [1,√2). Zero-length pairs at tied events are omitted.

08.Q3 · Stage 08

At t=1, t=1.2 and t=√2, what is β₁ under our endpoint convention?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

It is 1 at t=1, 1 at t=1.2, and 0 at t=√2, since [birth,death) includes its left endpoint and excludes the right.

08.Q4 · Stage 08

Why can an infinite death mean truncation rather than a permanent feature?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The algorithm knows only the supplied simplices and values. A class surviving to the largest computed threshold is unpaired there even if additional later simplices would kill it.

08.Q5 · Stage 08

What does multiplicity mean in a persistence diagram?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

More than one interval can have the same birth/death pair. They are separate classes even if a scatter plot draws their points on top of one another.

08.Q6 · Stage 08

Does a long interval identify one unique physical loop in the original data?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

No. A homology class contains many cycles differing by boundaries, and barcode bases and representatives need not be unique. Localizing a feature is an additional task.

08.Q7 · Stage 08

Explain why the pixel filtration has no essential H₁ after all pixels enter.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The union becomes the full rectangular image domain, which is contractible. Ordinary homology then has one H₀ class and no positive-dimensional classes.

08.Q8 · Stage 08

Why must the two real-data examples label their horizontal axes differently?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Iris uses a distance-based Rips construction; digits use a normalized intensity sublevel construction. Equal numerical values across those parameters have no intrinsic common geometric meaning.

09.Q1 · Stage 09

Trace the last edge column in the triangle-boundary example until it reduces to zero.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Start with {1,2}. XOR with {0,2} gives {0,1}. XOR with {0,1} gives the empty set. This is the cycle birth, subject to the later coface-pairing step.

09.Q2 · Stage 09

Why must faces precede cofaces at tied filtration values?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The filtration order must be compatible with inclusion. The boundary matrix needs each simplex’s faces available earlier; otherwise the matrix does not encode the required ordered chain complex.

09.Q3 · Stage 09

What does a zero reduced column mean, and how is its death found?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

It creates a class in the simplex’s dimension. Search whether its column index is the low row of a later reduced nonzero column. That later simplex supplies the death; absence of a pair gives infinite death within the input filtration.

09.Q4 · Stage 09

Why is adding columns by ordinary integer addition wrong over F₂?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Over F₂, 1+1=0. Ordinary integer addition produces 2 instead of cancellation. XOR or arithmetic modulo two is required.

09.Q5 · Stage 09

Which parameters must match before comparing two library diagrams?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Coefficient field, Rips distance versus radius convention, chosen complex, maximum homology dimension with sufficient cofaces, cutoff, treatment of infinite and zero-length intervals, and numerical comparison tolerance.

09.Q6 · Stage 09

Explain why a graph-only calculation can create a false infinite H₁ bar.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

A graph cycle can be killed by a triangle in the intended clique complex. Omitting all triangles removes the possible death operation, leaving a class that should not survive.

09.Q7 · Stage 09

What evidence do the independent H₀ merge calculation and direct Betti checks add?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

H₀ union-find checks merge scales using a different algorithm. Direct ranks check the number of live classes at event scales. Some common input construction remains shared, so the checks do not eliminate every common-mode error.

09.Q8 · Stage 09

Open the optional library-check report and explain the difference between SKIPPED and PASSED.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

SKIPPED means the required execution did not happen, for example because a package was unavailable. It supplies no evidence of agreement. PASSED means the named assertions were actually executed and succeeded.

10.Q1 · Stage 10

Find the bottleneck distance between a one-point diagram {(0,4)} and the empty diagram.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The cost is half the lifetime, so two. The best diagonal match is (2,2).

10.Q2 · Stage 10

Compare diagrams {(0,2)} and {(0.1,2.1)}: exhibit a matching of cost 0.1.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Match the two off-diagonal points directly. Both birth and death change by 0.1, so the maximum-norm cost is 0.1. Matching them separately to the diagonal costs at least one and is worse.

10.Q3 · Stage 10

Prove the sublevel-set inclusions from a uniform filtration-value bound δ.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

For any simplex with f(σ)≤t, g(σ)≤f(σ)+δ≤t+δ. Reverse the roles for the other inclusion. The induced homology maps give the corresponding shift relationships.

10.Q4 · Stage 10

Why does a pixel perturbation bound also bound the minimum value on shared faces?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

If |a_i−b_i|≤δ for all incident values, then min a_i≤min b_i+δ and min b_i≤min a_i+δ. Thus their minima differ by at most δ.

10.Q5 · Stage 10

Why are essential bars rejected by our finite-only distance function?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Infinity is not a finite coordinate and cannot be matched to the diagonal at finite cost. Silently removing such bars changes the problem. An extended-diagram method must specify their matching policy.

10.Q6 · Stage 10

Explain why a long barcode interval is not a statistical p-value.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Lifetime is a geometric/algebraic property of a declared filtration. A p-value needs a test statistic and a null distribution under stated assumptions. A long interval supplies neither by itself.

10.Q7 · Stage 10

Give an example where frame-level random splits would exaggerate model generalization.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Adjacent frames from one motion capture can be nearly identical. Training on some and testing on neighboring frames leaks session-specific information. Splitting by recording or participant better matches many deployment questions.

10.Q8 · Stage 10

What does the Stage 10 corruption experiment establish and not establish?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

It checks descriptor sensitivity and a fixed-grid stability bound under the declared bounded perturbation. It does not establish unseen-writer robustness, causal meaning, general statistical significance, or superiority to a baseline.

11.Q1 · Stage 11

Calculate a tent function for [0,2) at t=0, 0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The values are 0, 0.5, 1, 0.5, 0. Each is max(0,min(t,2−t)). It rises to the midpoint and falls back to zero.

11.Q2 · Stage 11

Explain the coordinate systems of an original digit image, a persistence diagram, and a persistence image.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The original image has spatial row/column coordinates with intensity values. The diagram has birth/death coordinates with interval multiplicity. The persistence image usually uses birth/lifetime coordinates with integrated weighted kernel mass.

11.Q3 · Stage 11

Why can a finite-interval count be unstable even when the diagram is stable?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Arbitrarily short bars near the diagonal can appear or disappear under small perturbations. The bottleneck cost can be tiny while the raw number of finite bars changes. A stable input descriptor does not make every discontinuous downstream statistic stable.

11.Q4 · Stage 11

What is the role of each of training, validation and test data?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Training fits parameters; validation selects among declared choices; test evaluates the finalized procedure. Repeatedly choosing based on test scores turns test into another validation set.

11.Q5 · Stage 11

Why is fitting a standardizer on all images before splitting a leakage problem?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The test distribution influences the learned means and scales used to train the model. Fitting transformations only on training rows better matches how unseen inputs are processed in deployment.

11.Q6 · Stage 11

Explain every step of the scalar-lens Mapper construction.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Choose a lens; cover its range; select observations in each cover element; cluster them in the chosen feature space; make a node per cluster; connect nodes sharing original sample IDs. Report cover and clustering parameters.

11.Q7 · Stage 11

When is the forward delay vector (x_t,x_(t+2),x_(t+4)) available?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Only at t+4, when the last value has arrived. Attributing the full vector to time t as immediately available would introduce future information.

11.Q8 · Stage 11

Why would equal or worse performance from topology still be an informative result?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

It identifies a limit of the method for that question and provides a valid baseline comparison. The goal is to learn whether shape descriptors add useful information, not guarantee a positive result.

12.Q1 · Stage 12

Write the complete worked-capstone question in one sentence without making a broad claim about all computer vision.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

For example: “On the frozen per-image split of the bundled digits snapshot, do the specified F₂ pixel-persistence summaries improve the declared logistic-regression baseline’s held-out image accuracy?” The scope includes the split and method.

12.Q2 · Stage 12

Why is it legitimate to refit on training plus validation before the final test?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The validation stage has finished model-choice work. Refitting the selected procedure uses all development data while the final test remains untouched. The scaler must also be refitted only on development data.

12.Q3 · Stage 12

List the frozen topological construction and feature choices.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Known normalization 1−image/16; closed nonperiodic square pixels; two-triangle subdivision preserving entry values; F₂; ordinary H₀/H₁; positive finite intervals for lifetime sums; fixed Betti thresholds 0,.25,.5,.75,1; 20 declared features; validation selection over the stated C values.

12.Q4 · Stage 12
