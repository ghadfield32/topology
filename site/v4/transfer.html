42 questions

00.T1 · Stage 00

Why is a 150 by 4 array not a statement that the underlying objects live on a four-dimensional manifold?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Array width counts selected features. Manifold dimension is a local structural property; neither dimension nor manifold assumptions follow from the table shape.

00.T2 · Stage 00

Would adding 1000 to every sample ID change your feature distances? Explain both the correct and incorrect implementations.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Correct: IDs are excluded, so distances do not change. Adding a common constant to an incorrectly included ID column also preserves its pairwise differences; that test alone does not expose the mistake. Arbitrarily permuting or rescaling IDs can expose it. IDs remain invalid measurement coordinates even if one translation test passes.

01.T1 · Stage 01

For A=[2], calculate kernel dimension over F2 and F3.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The map F2→F2 is zero, so its kernel has dimension one. Over F3 multiplication by two is invertible, so the kernel is zero-dimensional.

01.T2 · Stage 01

Why is a real-valued SVD threshold irrelevant to the exact F2 rank of a zero–one matrix?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

SVD approximates real singular values. Binary rank is a question about linear relations in a different field and uses exact modular arithmetic, not a floating-point tolerance.

02.T1 · Stage 02

Does multiplying all distances by three preserve the filtration at the same numeric threshold?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

No. The same complex appears after multiplying the threshold by three. Persistence coordinates scale, while the ordering of simplex appearances is preserved.

02.T2 · Stage 02

A feature table has two identical rows with different IDs. Which axiom fails when Euclidean distance is declared a metric on IDs?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Identity of indiscernibles: distinct IDs have zero distance. It is a pseudometric on IDs, although Euclidean distance remains a metric on distinct feature locations.

03.T1 · Stage 03

Why does P(x,y)=x have an inverse image operation even though it has no two-sided inverse?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The inverse image of a set collects every input whose output lies in that set; this is defined for any function. A two-sided inverse requires bijectivity.

03.T2 · Stage 03

Does a successful exhaustive finite-space check prove the analogous statement for all compact spaces?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

No. It verifies the enumerated finite example. An arbitrary-space statement needs its hypotheses and a general argument; infinite quantifiers are not exhausted by the finite test.

04.T1 · Stage 04

Does norm preservation guarantee that two transformations commute?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

No. Rotations and reflections can preserve norms while their composition order changes the result. The displayed R and F supply an explicit counterexample.

04.T2 · Stage 04

Why cannot the radial formula retract a full disk onto its circle boundary?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

It divides by ||x|| and is undefined at the center. A valid deformation must be continuous and defined at every point of the stated domain for every time.

05.T1 · Stage 05

For two diagonally touching selected pixels, why do a four-neighbor center graph and a closed-square union disagree about H0?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The graph omits a connection, while the closed squares share a corner. They encode different spaces; neither output alone resolves which representation the application needs.

05.T2 · Stage 05

Why does adding an interior diagonal to triangulate a square leave Euler characteristic unchanged?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Vertices are unchanged, edges increase by one, and the one square face is replaced by two triangle faces, increasing face count by one. The changes cancel in V−E+F.

06.T1 · Stage 06

Why must signed boundaries accompany an F3 rank routine?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The integer boundary uses alternating orientation signs. Those signs remain distinct modulo three. Omitting them can make boundary-of-boundary nonzero, so the purported homology quotient need not be defined.

06.T2 · Stage 06

For the integer map Z→Z, n↦2n, what are its kernel and cokernel?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The kernel is zero because 2n=0 implies n=0. The image is 2Z, and the cokernel is Z/2Z. The finite torsion quotient cannot be summarized by a positive free rank.

07.T1 · Stage 07

Can a Rips filtration on an equilateral three-point cloud have a positive-length H1 bar?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Not in the full clique construction under the stated edge-length convention: the triangle enters with its edges. A graph-only construction can retain the loop, but it is a different complex.

07.T2 · Stage 07

Why do we need (k+1)-simplices when computing Hk?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

They contribute the image of the next boundary map and can make k-cycles into boundaries. Omitting them may report classes that would be filled in the intended complex.

08.T1 · Stage 08

At t=√2, should the square H1 bar [1,√2) count toward beta1?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

No. The interval is half-open; the death event has occurred. The filled complex at that scale has no H1 class from the square outline.

08.T2 · Stage 08

Why can equal lifetimes describe different information?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Intervals [1,2) and [10,11) both last one unit but occupy different scales. A lifetime-only summary discards their positions in the filtration.

09.T1 · Stage 09

Does matching all H0 bars establish correctness of H1 persistence?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

No. H0 concerns connected-component merges. H1 involves cycles and filling by triangles; it needs its own controls and comparisons.

09.T2 · Stage 09

Why may tree edges differ while the persistence comparison still passes?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Tied edge lengths can permit multiple valid spanning trees. The finite death-time multiset, not the selected edge identities, is the invariant being compared.

10.T1 · Stage 10

A short diagram bar vanishes after a perturbation. Why can this still satisfy stability?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

The bar can be matched to the diagonal at half its lifetime in maximum norm. Stability controls an optimal matching, not preservation of every bar as an individually named object.

10.T2 · Stage 10

Does a long-lived stable interval imply a scientifically meaningful mechanism?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

No. Stability controls changes under a specified perturbation model. Meaning requires appropriate measurement choices, null or comparison models, and task-specific evidence.

11.T1 · Stage 11

Must a validation partition transformed by a training-fitted StandardScaler have zero mean?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

No. Only the fitted training distribution is centered according to its estimated means. Re-centering validation independently changes the fitted transformation and can hide distribution shift.

11.T2 · Stage 11

A forward window begins in 1900 and ends in 1902. When can it first be used by a past-only prediction system?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

At the availability time of the last included observation, not 1900. The example attaches year 1902, while real systems would also need publication or sensor arrival times.

12.T1 · Stage 12

Two model comparisons have the same net gain of four. Must their paired uncertainty calculations agree?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

No. Four gains and zero losses differ from 54 gains and 50 losses. Their discordance totals, conditional test distributions, and paired resampling distributions differ.

12.T2 · Stage 12

What would make a future follow-up confirmatory rather than another reanalysis of this example?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Freeze the study choices before inspecting genuinely new evaluation data, use a defensible sampling and split unit, and report the declared metrics and uncertainty without selecting a favorable outcome after the fact.

13.T1 · Stage 13

Why does a normalized point cloud lying on S2 not prove that the original scene lay on a sphere?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Normalization constructs unit directions and removes each point’s range. It maps many different physical points on a ray to the same direction.

13.T2 · Stage 13

Show that v=a−(a·p)p is tangent at a unit p.

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Take the dot product with p: p·v=p·a−(a·p)(p·p)=0 because p·p=1. This matches the derivative of the sphere constraint.

14.T1 · Stage 14

Can a wrong intrinsic matrix pass a project/unproject round trip?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Yes. Using the same invertible but incorrect matrix in both directions can cancel the error algebraically. Independent physical calibration needs separate evidence.

14.T2 · Stage 14

What distinguishes a reflection matrix from a proper rotation when both satisfy R.T R=I?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Their determinants: a proper rotation has +1; a reflection-type orthogonal transform has -1 and reverses orientation.

15.T1 · Stage 15

If the baseline is multiplied by 1000 but disparity and focal length are unchanged, what happens to reconstructed depth?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Depth is multiplied by 1000. The unit of depth follows the baseline; this error can preserve relative shape while ruining metric scale.

15.T2 · Stage 15

Why does a local Gaussian perturbation experiment not characterize gross correspondence failures?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

It perturbs values near a reference match under an imposed distribution. Incorrect matches can have large, structured, non-Gaussian errors and require direct algorithm evaluation.

16.T1 · Stage 16

Why does a softmax row summing to one not make it a calibrated correspondence-confidence distribution?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Normalization is a mathematical property of the operator. Calibration concerns agreement between predicted probabilities and real outcomes under evaluation, which this untrained example does not establish.

16.T2 · Stage 16

If every value is c, can attention produce a different vector under valid normalized weights?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

No. The weighted sum is c times the sum of weights, hence c. An output differing from c would expose an implementation or numerical problem under the stated conditions.

17.T1 · Stage 17

Can a global similarity transform correct arbitrary depth-dependent distortion?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

No. It has one scale, one rotation, and one translation. A locally varying deformation is outside that model even if some fitted anchors align well.

17.T2 · Stage 17

Why is evaluating the alignment on exactly its fitting anchors insufficient?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Optimization explicitly minimizes their residuals. Their agreement does not measure behavior at independent points or rule out overfitting, deformation elsewhere, or shared measurement errors.

18.T1 · Stage 18

How can a system with acceptable per-item inference time still display old results?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Queue wait, batching, transfer, decode, serialization, or display delay can dominate source-to-result age. Service time is only one portion of the path.

18.T2 · Stage 18

What must be checked before calling a vectorized implementation a valid optimization?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

It must preserve the intended numerical result, shapes, units, ordering and relevant metadata. Timings must then use comparable inputs and declared boundaries.

19.T1 · Stage 19

Can a confirmed event have an onset earlier than its decision timestamp?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Yes. The onset is a hypothesis about when the event began; confirmation may require later observations. The system must preserve the later decision time rather than pretending knowledge was available at onset.

19.T2 · Stage 19

Does passing prefix consistency establish event precision and recall?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

No. It establishes a past-only property of the implemented rule on the tested sequences. Precision and recall require independent reference labels and an explicit matching protocol.

20.T1 · Stage 20

A file checksum matches the saved learning record. What does that establish, and what does it not establish?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

It establishes byte identity with the recorded artifact, assuming the log itself is trusted. It does not establish mathematical correctness, independence of the attempt, understanding, or retention.

20.T2 · Stage 20

What is a productive response to discovering that a capstone result depends heavily on one arbitrary scale?

Reveal answer criteria after attempting

Report the sensitivity, revisit the measurement and metric rationale, and design a controlled comparison or new validation protocol. Do not silently choose the scale giving the most attractive result.
