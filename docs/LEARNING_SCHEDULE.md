# An efficient schedule with depth preserved

The original outline proposed 24 weeks at roughly 8–10 study hours per week. Treat this as a planning budget, not a promise. Stage 00 is orientation; each of Stages 01–12 can occupy two weeks. A faster learner can combine sessions after demonstrating the gates; a complete beginner may need more time with proofs and linear algebra.

For each stage, use four sessions: (1) intuition and definitions, (2) the hand-worked argument and exercises, (3) predictions and executable lab, and (4) independent assessment and error correction. Add a brief delayed recall session after about a week. Reading external material is targeted to a missed dependency or a major theorem’s proof, not an instruction to read five books simultaneously.

Keep a question log with the exact unknown word, failed step, or contradicted prediction. Use worked solutions after a genuine attempt. Re-answer with changed numbers or a changed assumption to show transfer rather than recognition.

A stage ends only when you can explain its key idea, state its definitions, work a small example, run and interpret its code, name a failure mode, and solve an unfamiliar question. Core software checks and the optional external-library gate are tracked separately from learner mastery.

Do not skip straight to the classifier because it produces a score. The bridge from boundaries to homology to persistence is the essential conceptual work; the final model is where those definitions are tested against a practical objective.
